THE SQUARE-ROOT RULE

Ten times the sample does not mean ten times the precision. The standard error scales with 1/√n, so the interval narrows by √10 ≈ 3.16×. That is why the 1M interval is 3.17× narrower, not 10×.

What changed

The 95% interval narrowed from 1.429 points to 0.451 points. The observed edge moved from 0.241% to 0.367%. The 1M interval no longer includes break-even.

This build uses the corrected visible-card insurance density. The card sequence is unchanged; insurance decisions at the threshold now use the pre-hole composition instead of conditioning on the hidden hole.

The first 100,000 rounds are not a new simulation. They are the same sequence as the 100k report: -241.00 units and 43,246–47,547–9,207.

What did not change

The rules, seed, cut card, chart and insurance threshold are identical. The exact edge remains 0.426%. The 1M point estimate sits 0.058 points below it, inside the interval.

Split EV still uses the documented independent-hand approximation. The chart is total-dependent, not composition-dependent.

What this does not prove

The simulation is a consistency check on the engine, not the source of the exact edge. A 1M interval of 0.142% to 0.593% is still ±0.226 points. Getting to ±0.05 points would take roughly 20.4 million rounds.
